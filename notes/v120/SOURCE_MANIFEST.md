# Sources for atlas v1.20

The Riemann Hypothesis is not proved. Citations below are the ones named in the audited notes. No further bibliographic entry was added.

The verbatim bundle, with its own file hashes, is `notes/v120/provenance/bundle/`. Its manifest is `notes/v120/provenance/bundle/SOURCE_MANIFEST.md`. Raw notes there are historical. `PUBLICATION_AUDIT_matching1983.md` and `AUDIT.md` override them. The superseded matching draft is not a source for public sentences.

## Citations

- A. Connes, Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, Selecta Math. 5 (1999); arXiv:math/9811068. Theorem VII.4 is the semilocal trace formula used as a citation.
- A. Connes and C. Consani, Weil positivity and trace formula, the archimedean place, arXiv:2006.13771 (2020). Theorems 1 and 3, cited as prior archimedean input.
- A. Weil, Sur les formules explicites de la théorie des nombres premiers (1952). Explicit formula and the positivity criterion.
- J. Tate, thesis (1950), in Cassels–Fröhlich (1967). Local zeta integrals.
- E. Haynsworth, Determination of the inertia of a partitioned Hermitian matrix, Linear Algebra Appl. 1 (1968). Bordered inertia.
- R. Hartshorne, Algebraic Geometry, GTM 52, Springer 1977, Chapter V, Theorem 1.9. Hodge index, cited as a sign pattern, not as a constructor. The harmonic-form sign is the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations; Huybrechts, Complex Geometry, or Griffiths and Harris, Principles of Algebraic Geometry, Chapter 0, are chapter-level pointers. Those pages were not re-opened here. Wikipedia is not the source.
- R. A. Rankin (1939) and A. Selberg (1940). Rankin–Selberg unfolding for Δ. Square coefficients, not an identity for W.
- S. Ramanujan (1916) and J.-P. Serre, A Course in Arithmetic. The weight-12 system is a calibration. It is not the archimedean counterterm.
- M. Auslander and D. Buchsbaum (1959). Regular local rings are unique factorization domains. The global counterexample Z[√−5] is classical.
- K. Iwasawa (1959). The growth formula for μ, λ, ν. Those invariants are not a sign.
- A. Weil (1948), Mattuck–Tate (1958), Grothendieck (1958). Hodge index and RH for curves over finite fields, already the finite-field side of the atlas. Not a map for ζ.

## Baseline

Public atlas v1.19, commit `65153b7d93b7314bd3f8359ac1c6c7e6f23fbba1`, 1025 concepts and 1990 relations. Confirmed on this branch before the v1.20 insertion.

An independent read of the parallel manuscript is archived at `notes/v120/provenance/INDEPENDENT_MATH_AUDIT.md`. It is not a new bibliography and it is not copied into the atlas as a paper. The atlas uses the items that change a public sentence: local means rather than a product-formula defect, E0 estimates rather than certified brackets, the h1 integral split, the band-limit reason a finite target fails, both parity sectors, and the Hartshorne locator. Earlier archive deposits announced as proofs of RH are not used.
