## Proposed parent/user report (under 700 words)

The promising part of the parabola analogy does survive an arithmetic calculation: **arithmetic selects a normalized boundary pole and a boundary polarity. What remains is extending that polarity to the complete interior with its sign controlled.**

**1. Constructed boundary, without zeros or a positivity assumption.** Write
\(\mathscr Z(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\).
The Gaussian theta/Mellin calculation gives residues −1 at 0 and +1 at 1, and the functional equation exchanges those points. Consequently the pole contribution to the explicit formula is evaluation at 0 plus evaluation at 1, each with coefficient one.
For \(\mathcal MF(s)=\int F(u)e^{(s-1/2)u}du\), these evaluations on a convolution square are exactly
\[
P(F)=2\operatorname{Re}(C_+(F)\overline{C_-(F)}).
\]
Thus the arithmetic boundary plane has matrix \(B_U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), and functional-equation reflection has that same matrix \(R\). Their product is positive: \(B_UR=I\).
The FE-fixed line is therefore positive, generated by \(e=h+v\), with \(B_U(e,e)=2\). Residue-normalized evaluation coordinates select this representative; abstractly FE selects its line, with an orientation still needed to choose its sign. This is a boundary-positive vector, not yet an ample class or an interior pole.

**2. Scaling distinguishes coordinate freedom from a new pole. [EXACT]** In the boundary basis associated to \((C_-,C_+)\), translation by \(b\) acts as
\(S_b=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-b/2},e^{b/2})\), with \(RS_bR=S_{-b}\).
Rescaling \(h\mapsto ah,v\mapsto a^{-1}v\) preserves the hyperbolic pairing but changes the matrix of the same FE to \(\begin{pmatrix}0&a^{-2}\\a^2&0\end{pmatrix}\). One cannot retain both that rescaling and an unchanged swap while claiming the same normalized arithmetic data.

**3. A complete independent analytic extension can be built—but is signed. [CONSTRUCTED/EXACT]** On the ordinary convolution algebra define a linear functional \(\tau\) from the prime-power orbit weights and the Gamma integral, with the original \(K_0\). Its convolution-square value is exactly \(P-W\). Therefore
\[
W(F,H)=\beta(F*H^*)-\tau(F*H^*),
\]
where \(\beta(g)=\mathcal Mg(0)+\mathcal Mg(1)\). This matches every prime term, Gamma term, diagonal subtraction and mixed term. It is an analytic coefficient construction, not a positive norm declared equal to \(W\). The local-trace interpretation is compatible with the earlier Connes calculation, but trace regularization does not preserve positivity automatically.

**4. The boundary cannot decide the interior sign. [EXACT/NUM]** I tested an explicit boundary-annihilating function:
\(G=(1-u^2)^4\) on [−1,1], \(F=G''-G/4\). Both moments vanish exactly. Numerical quadrature gives
\[
W(F)/\|F\|^2=0.002334.
\]
Deleting only the n=2 prime-power atom leaves the normalized boundary unchanged but gives approximately −0.63923 on this same function. This is a ghost control, not admissible alternative Euler data or a counterexample to any arithmetic theorem. It proves a boundary-only certificate would miss essential interior information.

**Conclusion.** Arithmetic selects the boundary pole line: yes. It selects the boundary annihilator and formal orthogonality: yes. A sign-controlled orthogonal sector reproducing full \(W\): not yet constructed. The precise missing structure is an independently positive extension of the boundary duality compatible with the signed local trace—not another choice of boundary coordinates. The next lemma to seek is a local-to-global positivity mechanism on the moment-annihilating convolution ideal that retains the exact Gamma constant and every prime-power weight. We have not assumed or proved it.

## Proofs and normalization details

### 1. Gaussian/Tate pole normalization [THM + EXACT]
Use standard additive character exp(2 pi i x), self-dual Lebesgue measure, and Gaussian exp(-pi x^2). Poisson summation [THM] gives theta(t)=t^(-1/2)theta(1/t), theta(t)=sum_(n in Z)exp(-pi n^2t).
For Re(s)>1,
Z(s)=(1/2) int_0^infty (theta(t)-1)t^(s/2)dt/t.
Splitting at 1 and substituting t->1/t below 1 yields
Z(s)=-1/s+1/(s-1)+(1/2)int_1^infty(theta(t)-1)[t^(s/2)+t^((1-s)/2)]dt/t.
The last integral is entire and symmetric. This proves the pole residues and FE directly, independently of nontrivial zeros or their location. The logarithmic derivative -Z'/Z has residue +1 at each pole; the poles therefore contribute +MF(0)+MF(1) in the explicit-formula convention used here. Z itself, not the entire xi=s(s-1)Z/2, is intended.
Tate's two residue functionals are an adelic extension of this theta model: evaluation at zero and integral, interchanged by the self-dual Fourier transform. We do not need to assume an adelic positive pairing to perform the above calculation.

Let A=C_c^infty(R), convolution (F*H)(u)=int F(v)H(u-v)dv, involution F*(u)=conj(F(-u)). Distinguish this superscript star from convolution multiplication. Mellin M(F*H)(s)=MF(s)MH(s), and M(F*)(s)=conj(MF(1-conj(s))).
Put C+=MF(1), C-=MF(0), beta(g)=Mg(0)+Mg(1). Then
beta(F*H*)=C-(F)conj(C+(H))+C+(F)conj(C-(H)).
This is the polarized boundary form, with coefficient exactly one per pole.

### 2. Boundary module and normalizations [CONSTRUCTED/EXACT]
Set U=Rh+Rv, B(h,h)=B(v,v)=0, B(h,v)=1, c(F)=C-(F)h+C+(F)v. Then beta(F*H*)=B(c(F),c(H)) (real version; sesquilinear complexification understood).
Reflection F(u)->F(-u) swaps moments, giving R h=v,R v=h. B(x,Ry) is the standard Euclidean positive metric in this residue basis. The +1 eigenline is Re, e=h+v; the -1 line R(h-v) has B-square -2. With norm-one convention e/sqrt(2) is selected up to sign; with evaluation coordinates the vector (1,1) is selected. The cone with both coordinates nonnegative chooses its sign. FE plus B without an orientation selects only the line, not a sign.
The shift T_bF(u)=F(u-b) gives c(T_bF)=S_b c(F), S_b=diag(exp(-b/2),exp(b/2)); S_b^T B S_b=B, R S_b R=S_-b. Scaling does not fix e: FE chooses the centered section of this orbit. No claim that e is invariant under the whole scaling group.
For A_a=diag(a,a^-1), the matrix of the same R in the new basis is A_a^-1 R A_a=[[0,a^-2],[a^2,0]]. Its fixed vector has new coordinates (a^-1,a), representing the old e. Requiring R still to be the bare swap forces a^2=1 over R. More generally the real B-isometries commuting with R are {I,-I,R,-R}; keeping pole labels and a positive orientation removes these residual discrete ambiguities.
There is no canonical splitting of the test-function quotient just from this: c:A->U is onto, ker c=E0, so A/E0 is the two-dimensional boundary module, but c does not select a compactly supported representative of each boundary class.

### 3. Independently specified convolution functional [CONSTRUCTED/EXACT]
For g in A define, with k(a)=e^(-a/2)/(1-e^(-2a)),
tau_inf(g)=-K0 g(0)-int_0^infty k(a)[2g(0)-g(a)-g(-a)]da,
tau_fin(g)=sum_(n=p^m) (log p)/sqrt(n) [g(log n)+g(-log n)],
tau=tau_inf+tau_fin,
K0=digamma(1/4)-log pi.
The integral converges: the symmetric bracket is O(a^2) at zero; k decays at infinity. The finite-place sum is locally finite for compact g. These specifications use the standard real Gamma factor and the logarithmic derivatives of the unramified Euler factors, not W's sign.
Indeed -d/ds log(1-p^-s)^-1=(log p)sum_(m>=1)p^-ms, whose critical weights at s=1/2 are (log p)p^-m/2; sign conventions here place these positive orbit coefficients in tau_fin.
The Gamma multiplier is independently recovered by the convergent digamma series:
K(t)=Re digamma(1/4+it/2)-log pi
 =K0+2 int_0^infty k(a)(1-cos(ta))da.
Expanding 1/(1-e^-2a) as a geometric series proves the equality termwise. This fixes K0 without a renormalization retuning.

For g=F*H*, g(0)=<F,H>, with the inner product linear in its first argument, and g(a)=<F,T_a H>. Symmetric translation differences give
int k(a)[2g(0)-g(a)-g(-a)]da
 =int k(a)<F-T_aF,H-T_aH>da.
Thus tau(F*H*)=-B_A(F,H), where B_A=W-P, including all cross terms. The formula W=beta-tau is now derived from independently specified local distributions, not a positivity declaration.
For support width A,
tau(F*F*)=M_A||F||^2-Jinf(F)-Jp(F),
M_A=-K0+2sum_(log n<A)Lambda(n)/sqrt(n).
This proves complete diagonal matching too. Individual jumps at b>A have correlation zero and their norm terms cancel their diagonal masses if included; use the original strict cutoff consistently. Infinite rank on each window remains; no finite-dimensional signature is inferred.

### 4. Full extended polarity and what is merely formal
Construct V=A direct-sum U, with J|U=B, J(h,F)=C+(F), J(v,F)=C-(F), J(F,H)=tau(F*H*).
This gives a fully specified arithmetic coefficient pairing using §3. Algebraically it is exactly the old formal bordered model, not a new geometric polarization.
Define pi F=F-C-(F)h-C+(F)v. Then J(pi F,U)=0 and
J(pi F,pi H)=tau(F*H*)-beta(F*H*)=-W(F,H).
Hence V=U orthogonal-direct-sum pi(A), and
e^perp=R(h-v) orthogonal-direct-sum pi(A),
J|e^perp=-2 on h-v, direct-sum -W on pi(A).
There are two distinct notions of primitive sector: E0=ker c within the original function space, and pi(A)=U^perp in the extension. They should not be conflated. FE makes B_U R positive only on U. On pi(A), taking reflection as the extension does not supply a sign theorem. A Cartan operator that were +1 on e and -1 on e^perp would give J(x,Theta x)>0 only if the missing sign already held. Defining that operator algebraically is not constructing an independently positive metric.
